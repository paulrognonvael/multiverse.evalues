Major comments:

1.      I have a feeling that the weighted sum-to-zero coding in Section 2 is incorrect. As defined, gik = ρk for observations in subpopulation k and −(1 − ρk) otherwise, so the column sum is nkρk − (n − nk)(1 − ρk) = n(ρk² − (1 − ρk)²) = n(2ρk − 1), which is zero only when ρk = 1/2. The claim that Σi gik = 0, and hence the identity that βj(l) equals the average effect fails. The intended coding is presumably gik = 1 − ρk for observations in subpopulation k and −ρk otherwise, which does sum to zero. The subsequent expression for βjk(l) must be corrected accordingly. Please verify whether the code implements the stated or the corrected coding.

2.      The simulations never evaluate the multiverse setting the paper is motivated by. There is no simulation with L outcomes and px treatments, correlated tests, a mix of null and non-null hypotheses, and e-BH applied to the ensemble.

3.      The obvious p-value-based competitor is missing. Benjamini-Yekutieli also controls FDR under arbitrary dependence and requires no nuisance parameter estimation. The paper compares e-value strategies only against each other. A comparison of e-BH against BY is needed

4.      Type I error assessment (as in Table 1) is based on 100 repetitions, which gives a Monte Carlo standard error of roughly 0.02 at α = 0.05. At least 1000 repetitions are needed for the type I error rows.

5.      This framework is described in terms of treatments and average treatment effects, and the derivative of the link-transformed mean is called an ATE, but (a) in the logistic case this is a conditional log-odds coefficient, not an ATE in the usual causal sense, and (b) the MCS application is observational, with the authors themselves describing findings as associations in the abstract.
